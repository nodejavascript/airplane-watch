Watch a named aircraft leave a named airport

Pick an airport, point a fence at it, and name the aircraft you care about by tail number or callsign. The page reads a public flight feed every ten seconds and tells you the moment one of yours is airborne — with a browser notification if you want one. Everything runs in this tab. There is no account, no key, and nothing to install.

  • It is honest about the hard part. An aircraft sitting on the apron is only heard by a receiver a few kilometres away, so a departure is usually detected seconds after it is airborne, not at the moment of rotation. Every row says which of the two you got, in its phase column.
  • Your watchlist stays in your browser. Tail numbers you type are matched locally and never sent here or to Google.
  • Free and open data. The feed is adsb.lol, published under the Open Database License.

    Destination is looked up, not heard.     What this cannot see — read this before you rely on it

    • It cannot tell you the wheels are leaving the ground. It tells you an aircraft that was in the fence is airborne. Surface coverage from a receiver far away is poor, so the usual case is a departure noticed a few seconds late. Each row is marked as seen on the ground first (a fact) or first seen climbing (a strong inference).
    • Only aircraft that transmit ADS-B appear. Aircraft that have asked not to be published, and older aircraft that only carry Mode S, are either absent or arrive without a position. An aircraft missing from this page is not an aircraft that is not flying.
    • A missing altitude is not an aircraft on the ground. Some transponders send position without altitude, and those readings are shown as no altitude and are never used to decide a departure.
    • A flyover is not a departure. An aircraft already known to be airborne that enters the fence is ignored, however low it is — which means a genuine departure from a neighbouring airfield, climbing through the fence, will not be reported. That is the deliberate cost of not crying wolf.
    • The fence is a circle, and reception is not. A receiver's range depends on the aircraft's altitude, the terrain and the weather, so what the page shows you is what one network could hear, not what is up there. Distances you choose are in kilometres; the feed itself works in nautical miles, which is 1.852 km, and the conversion is exact rather than approximate.
    • The free feed rate-limits, and it says so. Asking too often is answered with HTTP 429, which the page reports rather than hiding. The proxy in front of it caches for a few seconds so a crowd does not multiply the load. Measured 20 Sep 2026: seven airports polled back to back had five refused by the third round.
    • Positions are approximate and delayed. The feed updates in seconds, and the map-precision of a position derived by multilateration is worse than one the aircraft reported itself.

    How it works

    1. Where the position comes from. Aircraft broadcast their identity, position and altitude over 1090 MHz. Volunteers with a cheap receiver and an antenna hear those broadcasts and send them to adsb.lol, which republishes the merged picture for anyone to read.
    2. Why there is a proxy in the middle. The feed sends no cross-origin headers, so a browser is not allowed to read it directly — the request goes out, the answer comes back, and the browser discards it. Every call from this page therefore goes to /api/… on this domain, and a small proxy passes it on and adds the header the browser needs.
    3. How a departure is decided. Once every ten seconds the page asks for every aircraft within the distance you chose. Each reading is classified as on the ground, airborne, or unknown, and compared with the same aircraft's previous reading. Ground to airborne inside the distance is a departure; seen only after it is already climbing is a probable one; already airborne is ignored; a stale reading proves nothing. A match against one of your rules is what marks a row as watched, and what makes the page tell you when that aircraft leaves.
    4. Where it is kept. Nowhere on a server. The watchlist, the chosen airport and your place name live in this browser's own storage and are removed when you clear site data.

    Privacy

    This policy governs aircraft-demo.nodejavascript.com, and it is written from what the code does rather than from a template. It was last changed on 20 September 2026.

    • There is no account and no server-side store. Nothing you type is sent to this site. Your watchlist, your chosen airport, the aircraft types you picked and the distance you set are kept in your own browser's storage and are never transmitted anywhere.
    • Google Analytics loads only after you say yes. Google's script is not in the page. It is added by consent.js when you press Accept all, and if you press Reject all then no request is made to Google at all and no cookie is set. What it would record is anonymous: the page you are on, which controls you press, how far down you scroll, and which airport and radius you chose. It never records a tail number, a callsign or anything else you type.
    • The only cookie is the analytics one, and it exists only if you accepted. Your answer itself is remembered in your browser's local storage, not in a cookie — setting a cookie to record a cookie decision would be its own small joke.
    • You can change your answer at any time with the Cookie settings button in the footer. Rejecting after accepting reloads the page so that counting genuinely stops.
    • What leaves this machine while the page is open: a request every ten seconds to /api/… on this domain, which is passed to the third-party feed adsb.lol. That request contains the airport and radius you chose and your IP address, and no identifier for you. The feed's own terms and licence are the Open Database License, and its site explains what it does with requests.
    • Aircraft data is about aircraft, not about people. The feed carries no passenger information, and none is available from it.
    • Notifications are sent by your own browser, from your own machine, and only if you pressed the button that asks for permission. Declining them changes nothing else on the page.
    • To contact anyone about this site, or to ask what is held about you: the answer is nothing, and the way to prove it is to clear this site's data in your browser. Questions can be raised through the contact form on nodejavascript.com.
